About

I work at the boundary between magnonics and quantum circuits: coupling spin waves to microwave photons in resonators I design and fabricate myself, then extending that hands-on RF background into superconducting-qubit readout hardware. The direction I want to take into a PhD is circuit elements that borrow spiking, neuron-like dynamics for computation without giving up quantum behavior — hardware that computes more like a brain and still runs on Hilbert space.

Research

Two projects, one thread: microwave circuits that couple to something quantum.

Cavity Magnonics Thesis

An original elliptical spiral resonator design — the Archimedean Spiral Double Split-Ring Resonator (A-SDSRR), grown out of a 2024 split-ring resonator paper — built to couple magnons to microwave photons. The full loop runs simulation in CST Microwave Studio, fabrication on FR4 by toner transfer and wet etching, and characterization on a Rohde & Schwarz ZNLE18 vector network analyzer.

g/2π ≈ 65–70 MHz — coupling peaks near 3 spiral turns, not the highest turn count

Boards fabricated and measured across five turn counts (N = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9). First-author paper covering the theory, CST modeling, and VNA measurements.

Superconducting Qubit Readout

In progress — geometry engine phase

Reproducing the multiplexed readout circuit from a 2025 PRX Quantum paper on multiconductor-transmission-line qubit readout: a 16-qubit device with transmons, MTL-coupled readout and filter resonators, and TSV-routed shared readout lines. Building it in code on Quantum Metal, starting with the physics and geometry engine and the core MTL-coupler unit before qubits, tiling, or the TSV backside structure.
